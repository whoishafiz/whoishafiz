Earlier research

Volume-Time Equivalence: sonoluminescence bubble collapse

Paper draft
  • Derives a volume–energy relation from mass–energy equivalence by writing mass as density × volume and the speed term as wavelength × frequency. The result is dimensionally consistent.
  • Tested against Keller–Miksis bubble-dynamics simulations, sweeping drive pressure, frequency, starting radius and gas to check that the predicted collapse timescale tracks the simulated one.
  • Maps where the relation applies: it holds where a volume truly collapses under an independently sourced energy, and reduces to an identity for linear oscillators.

  • PhD, Systems EngineeringUniversiti Brunei Darussalam · Jan 2023 – presentResearch overview & timeline

    Research overview

    Lithium-ion batteries fade with every charge cycle. Knowing a cell's state of health (SoH) and how many cycles it has left, its remaining useful life (RUL), lets equipment be serviced before it fails rather than after. My PhD develops deep learning models that predict both from a cell's cycling history, and the data and evaluation practices that make those predictions trustworthy.

    PublishedData integrity first

    A preprocessing step that screens measured battery data with digit-frequency (Benford) analysis, flagging outliers and logging errors before they bias a model.

    Under reviewSequential model optimisation

    Dual-output recurrent networks (LSTM / GRU) for SoH and RUL, tuned systematically and evaluated on cells the model never saw in training, with every run repeated over several seeds.

    In developmentContinual learning

    A VAE plus GRU model with a replay buffer, so it can keep learning from new battery datasets without forgetting the ones it learned before.

    In preparationCritical review

    A review of data-driven battery prognostics centred on the pipeline: how data are split, scaled and evaluated, and where published results can overstate accuracy.
